What is Plagiarism?

Understanding the boundaries of intellectual property and common knowledge.

Textbook Briefing (Unit 1.4)

In global academia, ideas are treated as private property belonging to the originating creator. Plagiarism means taking ideas, words, or data without giving clear credit. However, common knowledge (facts widely known and accepted) does not require a citation.

Types of Plagiarism

Differentiating between intentional malpractice, patchworking, and accidental mistakes.

Textbook Briefing (Unit 1.4 / Degrees of Plagiarism)

Academic systems classify violations to assign proportional responses. Copy-pasting chunks of prose and replacing single words is called **patchworking** (often tied to developing writing fluency). **Collusion** involves presenting peer collaboration as purely individual work.

Referencing Mechanics (APA 7th)

Mastering APA 7th editions, abbreviations in citations, secondary references, and bibliography structure.

Textbook Briefing (APA 7th Standards)

The **APA 7th edition** system governs in-text citations and reference lists. Key components:

  • Book Reference: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of book in italics (Edition). Publisher. (Note: No place of publication is listed in the 7th edition!).
  • In-text abbreviations: Use "et al." immediately from the first citation for three or more authors. Do not use *ibid.* or *op. cit.*—these are not used in APA style.
  • Secondary References: When citing a source you read within another source, name the original but cite the one you actually read: e.g., McEwan (2015, as cited in Kelman, 2016).
  • Reference List vs. Bibliography: A Reference List only includes works cited in your text. A Bibliography contains background or consulted material not directly cited.

Quoting & Combining Sources

Integrating external voices cleanly while building cohesive, multi-source arguments.

Textbook Briefing (Units 1.8 & 1.9)

When quoting under APA 7th, always specify page numbers (e.g., p. 45 or pp. 45–46). Quotes with 40 or more words must be formatted as **block quotations** (indented 0.5 inches, double-spaced, no quotation marks). When synthesizing, use logical connectives to bridge opposing views.

Paraphrasing Builder

Applying the three pillars of text transformations: Synonyms, Word Class, and Word Order.

Textbook Briefing (Unit 1.7)

A paraphrase must reflect a significant change in structural layout and vocabulary while keeping the exact logic of the original source text. Master paraphrasing by:

  • Lexical Substitution: Generating synonyms (e.g., *hesited* → *demonstrated reluctance*).
  • Word Class Change: Transmuting nouns to verbs, or adjectives to nouns (e.g., *hesitation* [noun] → *hesitated* [verb]).
  • Word Order/Voice: Reorganizing active clauses to passive layouts.

APA 7th Guidelines
  • Always list the publisher directly without the geographical city.
  • Utilize the serial (Oxford) comma before "and" in listing authors inside the References block.
  • Provide DOIs as active URLs formatted as https://doi.org/xxxx.
Final Step Checklist
  • Always compile full source metadata *during* the initial reading stage.
  • Ensure that any in-text direct citations have corresponding page numbers.
  • When paraphrasing, first look away from the text and explain the concept out loud before writing. This helps preserve your own natural voice.
