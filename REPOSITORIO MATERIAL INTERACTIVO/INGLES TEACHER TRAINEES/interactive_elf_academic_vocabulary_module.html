Unit 1: Register Translation

Formal vs. Informal Lexis

Theoretical Framework:

Academic register is characterized by its high density of information, objectivity, and precision. EAP instruction emphasizes replacing phrasal verbs, vague descriptors, and emotionally charged vocabulary with single-word Latinate lexical items (Ali Saeed, 2022).

Unit 2: Abbreviation Syntax

Academic Abbreviations & Acronyms

Theoretical Framework:

Abbreviations falling under Latin formulas (i.e., e.g., cf., et al.) conserve space. They require meticulous formatting under APA style. For instance, Latin abbreviations must be lowercase, punctuated with internal periods, and accompanied by proper commas when used parenthetically.

Unit 3: Structural Shift

Academic Nouns & Nominalization

Theoretical Framework:

Nominalization is the transformation of verbs and adjectives into nouns. This increases density and objectifies discourse by removing immediate human agents. Example: "We implemented" $\rightarrow$ "The implementation of."

🧩 Task: Complete the nominalized transform by matching the correct nominal equivalents for each dynamic part.

Unit 4: Lexical Precision

Academic Adjectives

Theoretical Framework:

Adjectives classify (*qualitative*, *systemic*) or evaluate (*substantial*, *unsubstantiated*). Academic registers avoid conversational intensity (e.g., *amazing*, *terrible*, *huge*) in favor of scholarly precision and measured tones.

💡 Task: Match the informal adjectives to their corresponding precise, academic equivalents.

Unit 5: Rhetorical Force

Academic Verbs & Reporting Stance

Theoretical Framework:

Reporting verbs signify the writer's attitude towards the claim they cite. Selecting *demonstrates* signals full alignment with a proven fact, while selecting *contends* labels the citation as an argumentative viewpoint.

✍️ Task: Choose the precise reporting verb that matches the author's intended rhetorical stance.

Stance: Full Agreement (Fact-backed)
"Chomsky (1965) that human beings possess an innate device."
Stance: Neutral / Objective Exposition
"Krashen (1982) that acquisition occurs through input exposure."
Stance: Doubt / Non-factual Perspective
"The traditionalist school that native accentuation is the only goal."
Unit 6: Statement Calibration

Academic Adverbs & Hedging

Theoretical Framework:

Hedging is the practice of limiting commitment to statements to preserve scholarly accuracy. Modifying absolute claims (e.g., changing *always* to *frequently*) avoids academic overgeneralization.
