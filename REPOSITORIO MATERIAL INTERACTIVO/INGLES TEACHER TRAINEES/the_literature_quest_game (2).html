Archivist's Reference Notes

Directive 1 • Why study Literature in EFL?

Importance of Literature

Literature builds language and communication skills, helps expand vocabulary, encourages wild imagination, and builds intense critical thinking tools. Reading works from different historical periods introduces us to foreign cultures, perspectives, and belief systems.

Directive 2 • The Architecture of Genres

Prose vs Poetry

Prose: Written language in standard grammatical flow (novels, short stories, news). It consists of paragraphs and complete sentences.

Poetry: Imaginative language using formal meter, rhyme scheme, or free verse to invoke complex emotions.

Genres subdivisions: Narrative, Lyric (personal emotion), and Dramatic (plays meant to be performed with dialogue and monologues).

Directive 3 • Point of View & Irony

POV & Irony Rules

Point of view defines how stories are related: 1st Person (Participant narrator), 2nd Person (direct appeal), and 3rd Person (Omniscient / Limited views).

Narrator Reliability: A gap can occur between narrator claims and reality. This produces Dramatic Irony when characters intend outcomes that readers know will fail due to underlying parameters.

Directive 4 • Character & Setting Dynamics

Presentation of Self

Telling: Informing the reader directly about traits. Showing: Leaving it up to readers' interpretations of clues (Dress, speech patterns, social standings, actions, and reactions).

Setting: The time, atmosphere, custom rules, beliefs, and places that generate social situations and control the characters' psychological situation.

Directive 5 • Plot Arcs & Theme Clues

Plot and Themes

Plot Mountain: Exposition -> Rising Action -> Climax -> Falling Action -> Resolution.

Plot Layouts: Linear (Chronological), Episodic (Loosely related incidents tied by theme), Parallel (Woven stories colliding at a main climax), and Flashback (Action starting in media res, tracing back in time).

How to identify themes: Examine book titles, descriptive space density, symbol motifs, and direct authorial intrusions.
